# USB port interconnect screen

The concept assembly places its assumed USB-C port at `[84, 0, 2.8, 12, 6, 3]` mm and its main PCB at `[92, 12, 3.2, 78, 55, 0.8]` mm. Their boxes overlap in x and z but have a **6 mm y gap**. The port cannot be soldered directly to that main board as modeled. A released design needs a revised board edge, a daughterboard, or a flex and a complete pin map, retention scheme, enclosure opening, seal, and assembly process. No such interconnect is present.

At the PCB height, the port and board overlap over only **4 mm in x**. The AABB corridor `[92, 6, 3.2, 4, 6, 0.8]` mm is clear of the other current concept solid envelopes, but a 4 mm corridor does not establish a connector footprint, contact fanout, bend radius, signal integrity, or a mount. Extending a carrier under the full assumed 12 mm port width produces `[84, 6, 3.2, 12, 6, 0.8]` mm and intersects the current battery-pack envelope by **7 × 1 × 0.8 mm**. Shifting the port right to start at the PCB x edge (92 mm) would gain a 12 mm corridor, but **8 mm of the port would lie outside the frame's existing 12 mm cutout**. These are envelope screens of assumed geometry, not manufactured-part collision or tolerance results. A viable layout must revise the pack, board, port, or chassis together and then prove the actual connector and flex geometry.

Run `python -m buildslate.usb_fit` to recompute the gap and routing envelopes from the concept assembly and screen a public connector candidate. The [Hardware view](https://superflyad.github.io/BuildSlate/?tab=hardware) publishes the result. Box contact would only remove this specific separation; it would not prove a footprint, connector mating axis, cable route, electrical operation, or enclosure fit.

The [Amphenol 12402075E512A product page](https://www.amphenol-cs.com/product/12402075e512a.html) lists an active USB4 Gen 3 Type-C mid-mount receptacle, 24 contacts, 5 A current rating, and 0.8 mm PCB thickness. The [detailed product specification SUSB013 Rev C](https://cdn.amphenol-cs.com/media/wysiwyg/files/documentation/ps-susb013.pdf) lists a **20 V maximum operating voltage**. The page's separate 100 V field conflicts with that lower operating limit; the screen uses 20 V pending supplier clarification. Its [mechanical drawing](https://cdn.amphenol-cs.com/media/wysiwyg/files/drawing/c12402075.pdf) is linked for a future footprint and CAD review, but its dimensions have not been incorporated into the current port box.

At 20 V and 5 A, the connector's simple voltage-current ceiling is 100 W. The aspirational 100 W wired input sits at that ceiling before connector heating, cable rating, PD policy, PCB copper, charger losses, system loads, and the battery's unknown charge curve are considered. The EVT0 30 W input cap is a separate provisional limit, not permission to energize an unbuilt circuit. The connector is a **candidate**, not a selected or qualified part, and does not make the USB or charging path buildable.
