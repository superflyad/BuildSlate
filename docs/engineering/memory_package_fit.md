# 128 GB memory package screen

The current board has four 14 × 10 mm planning sites, each labeled 32 GB. They express the approved 128 GB capacity target, not selected DRAM geometry. [Samsung lists a 32 GB LPDDR5X capacity](https://semiconductor.samsung.com/dram/lpddr/lpddr5x/), and [Micron announced an intended 8–32 GB range](https://investors.micron.com/news/press-release/2025/Micron-Ships-Worlds-First-1-1-Gamma-Based-LPDDR5X-Enabling-Rich-Mobile-AI-Experiences-06-03-2025/default.aspx). No reviewed current-production 32 GB part with X2 qualification fits these sites.

The observed X2 Elite Extreme packaging differs from this board hypothesis. [ASUS lists 48 GB LPDDR5X memory on package](https://www.asus.com/laptops/for-home/zenbook/asus-zenbook-a16-ux3607/techspec/) for its Zenbook A16 X2 configuration, and [TechInsights' physical analysis](https://www.techinsights.com/ja/node/61888) reports three co-packaged 16 GB DRAM packages. The four-site and eight-site studies below therefore do not represent that commercial X2 package. They remain external-memory geometry tests pending a supplier-supported 128 GB package architecture.

Micron's exact [`MT62F4G64DAFL-023 WT:C`](https://www.micron.com/products/obsolete/obsolete-lpddr5x/part-catalog/part-detail/mt62f4g64dafl-023-wt-c) is a **256 Gb = 32 GB** LPDDR5X package, 14 × 12.4 × 0.96 mm, with 496 balls and an x64 bus. Micron marks it **obsolete**. Four would reach 128 GB by capacity, but none fits a 14 × 10 mm planning site in either rotation. The 0.96 mm height is below the 1.0 mm nominal block height by only 0.04 mm before solder and tolerances. Four independent x64 host connections need 256 data bits against the X2 Extreme's published 192-bit bus. This rejects the current four-site direct-wiring proposal; a supplier-supported shared-channel or other topology is unproven. The part is a rejected geometry reference, not a sourcing or design selection.

For a concrete comparison, Micron lists [`MT62F2G64D8EK-023 WT:C`](https://www.micron.com/products/memory/lpddr-components/lpddr5x/part-catalog/part-detail/mt62f2g64d8ek-023-wt-c) as a 128 **gigabit** (16 **gigabyte**) LPDDR5X part in a 14 × 14 × 1.1 mm, 441-ball, x64 package. Eight such parts would total 128 GB. The current board reserves four sites of 14 × 10 mm and the assembly uses 1.0 mm nominal package height, so this exact package does not fit even one planning site. Eight package outlines alone would occupy 1,568 mm², compared with 560 mm² reserved by the four rectangles. This is an area and outline screen, not a proposed eight-package layout.

Micron's [LPDDR5X part catalog](https://in.micron.com/products/memory/dram-components/lpddr5x/part-catalog) also lists exact `MT62F2G64D8CZ-023 WT:C` as **Production**, with 128 Gb (16 GB), x64, 561 balls, and an 8 × 12.4 × 1.2 mm package. Rotated to 12.4 × 8 mm, its outline fits each 14 × 10 mm planning rectangle. Its 1.2 mm body exceeds the current 1.0 mm modeled height by 0.2 mm before solder and tolerance. Eight outlines total 793.6 mm², 233.6 mm² beyond the four reserved rectangles, and still require four added sites. The separate [compact eight-package screen](../../engineering/pcb/slate-pocket-v1r-floorplan/memory-8x16-compact-study.yaml) places four on each board side at assumed coordinates: the nearest screened planar gap is 2.0 mm under a 1.0 mm assumed rule. This establishes an improved nominal outline route, not a land pattern, electrical map, current procurement, or a buildable board. The separate [45-solid editable compact STEP](../../cad/slate-pocket-v1r/concept-cad/memory-8x16-compact.step) and [public 3D proof](https://superflyad.github.io/BuildSlate/?model=compact8&tab=proof) place those exact outlines in the full assembly. The earlier 45-solid memory variant still depicts the larger 14 × 14 mm reference. Both variants retain unresolved height, topology, routing, and supplier evidence.

Qualcomm's [X2 Elite brief](https://www.qualcomm.com/content/dam/qcomm-martech/dm-assets/documents/Snapdragon-X2-Elite-Product-Brief.pdf) lists a 192-bit memory bus for the X2 Elite Extreme. Eight independent x64 package connections would need 512 data bits, so that direct wiring arrangement is unavailable. This **does not prove** that eight packages are impossible: a supplier-supported shared-channel, rank, or other attachment could use the controller differently. The public brief does not establish such a topology, its timing, or its routing. The package screen publishes the width comparison and checks the quoted 192-bit width against Qualcomm's 9,523 MT/s and 228 GB/s figures. This calculation is an inference from the two manufacturer specifications, not a qualified board design.

The [X2 Elite](https://www.qualcomm.com/content/dam/qcomm-martech/dm-assets/documents/Snapdragon-X2-Elite-Product-Brief.pdf) and [X2 Plus](https://www.qualcomm.com/content/dam/qcomm-martech/dm-assets/documents/Snapdragon-X2-Plus-Product-Brief.pdf) briefs give different widths for the reviewed SKUs. The screen now checks each width against its published rate and bandwidth, then compares both exact Micron reference-package routes:

| X2 SKU | Controller bus | Four 32 GB x64 packages, independent host width | Eight 16 GB x64 packages, independent host width |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| X2E-96-100 | 192 bits | 256 bits, 64 over | 512 bits, 320 over |
| X2E-90-100 | 128 bits | 256 bits, 128 over | 512 bits, 384 over |
| X2P-64-100 | 128 bits | 256 bits, 128 over | 512 bits, 384 over |
| X2P-42-100 | 128 bits | 256 bits, 128 over | 512 bits, 384 over |

These widths reject one independent full-width host connection per package on every reviewed X2 SKU, including the compact 16 GB x64 part. The 32 GB reference is obsolete; the 14 × 14 mm 16 GB reference is listed as contact sales; the compact 16 GB reference is cataloged as Production, with procurement still unverified. Sharing data lanes across ranks, using another package arrangement, or selecting memory on package would need an exact supplier-supported map and an orderable 128 GB configuration. The arithmetic does not establish that any such configuration is available or electrically valid.

NVIDIA's [Jetson T5000 module datasheet, revision 1.5](https://developer.nvidia.com/downloads/assets/embedded/secure/jetson/thor/docs/jetson-thor-series-modules-datasheet_ds-11945-001.pdf) documents a real 128 GB arrangement of eight 16 GB LPDDR5X packages on a 256-bit module bus. It does not publish a Qualcomm X2 channel/rank map or qualify the exact Micron reference packages used in this screen. The T5000's published 15.29 mm module height also exceeds Slate's complete 9.5 mm body. This example establishes that package count alone does not determine host bus width; it does not resolve Slate's memory integration.

## Eight-package outline study

The separate [placement study](../../engineering/pcb/slate-pocket-v1r-floorplan/memory-8x16-study.yaml) tests four 14 × 14 mm packages on the component side and four at matching locations on the reverse side. Against the current non-memory planning blocks, the component boundary, and an **assumed** 1.0 mm planar gap rule, the closest screened gap is 1.5 mm. The reverse side has no other components in the present floorplan. This establishes only that the eight package *boxes* can be drawn inside the board planform in that arrangement; it is not an X2 memory-on-package layout.

The floorplan, mechanical board envelope, and compute-zone stack now use the same provisional 0.8 mm PCB thickness. The board top stays at z = 4.0 mm; reverse-side package bottoms move to z = 2.1 mm. This resolves an internal drawing mismatch, not fabrication feasibility. The study still does not test land patterns, bottom-side assembly, vias and escape, decoupling, shielding, heat flow, actual memory-controller topology, or a booted device. The eight boxes replace the four planning solids only in the separate [45-solid STEP alternative](../../cad/slate-pocket-v1r/concept-cad/memory-8x16.step) and its matching public 3D view. The baseline remains unchanged. Both exports have independent CAD proof reports.

## ADATA source unit reconciliation

ADATA's [US LPDDR5X listing](https://www.adata.com/us/embedded/lpddr5x/) labels `L5CG32D2023MCAS` as **32GB (1024M x32)** and `L5EG32D4023MCAS` as **64GB (2048M x32)**. The [UK listing for the same MPNs](https://www.adata.com/uk/embedded/lpddr5x/) calls them **32Gb** and **64Gb**. The US organization fields resolve to 32 and 64 gigabits, or **4 and 8 gigabytes per package**. The US GB labels therefore cannot establish 32 or 64 GB package capacity. Four packages would provide 16 or 32 GB, not 128 GB.

At those reconciled capacities, 128 GB would need 32 or 16 packages, respectively. One independent x32 connection per package would need 1024 or 512 data bits, above the reviewed X2 Elite Extreme's 192-bit and X2 Plus's 128-bit buses. A supported shared-channel or on-package topology has not been supplied. Both ADATA outlines are 12.4 × 15 mm and miss the present 14 × 10 mm planning rectangles in either orientation. The public page does not provide a package height or establish X2 qualification, orderability, or a working design. The [Hardware view](https://superflyad.github.io/BuildSlate/?tab=hardware) publishes the source reconciliation and arithmetic.

Run `python -m buildslate.memory_package_fit` to recompute the comparison, bus screen, ADATA unit check, and outline study from the profile, public part records, board floorplan, and assembly. The check rejects confusion between gigabits and gigabytes, an internally inconsistent X2 bus specification, false clearances, and promotion of planning sites to verified geometry.

The 128 GB floor remains unchanged. Closing the gap requires either a qualified exact 32 GB package and reviewed four-package topology or a new eight-package electrical and mechanical design. Both routes need Qualcomm's supported X2 channel and rank map, memory rails, signal rules, package drawings, board routing, boot firmware, power and thermal tests, and the measured [resident-memory exercise](compute_platform_decision.md#resident-memory-exercise) on a serialized Slate unit. A public controller maximum is insufficient.
